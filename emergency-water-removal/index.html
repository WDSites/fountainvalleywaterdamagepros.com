When standing water is an emergency

Emergency water removal in Fountain Valley is the fast extraction of standing water from floors, carpet and low areas before it reaches walls, cabinets and subfloors. A flooded kitchen, a laundry room that overflowed and a bathroom that ran all night all qualify.

Each hour matters. Water moves into drywall by wicking upward, soaks carpet pad within minutes and slips under flooring along seams, so the volume you can see is rarely the whole problem.

The city sits low and flat, at roughly 33 feet above sea level, and a flat slab lets spilled water spread outward through a room and under walls. That is one practical reason crews move quickly to pull water out before it travels.

Safety first: electricity and contaminated water

Some water is dangerous before it is damaging. Read these steps before you wade in.

  • If water touches outlets, appliances, the electrical panel or any gas equipment, stay out and call the utility or 911 before calling us.
  • Turn off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Never stand in water to flip a breaker.
  • If the water came from a toilet, sewer drain or outside flooding, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Lift valuables, electronics and paper onto counters, tables or blocks.

Burst pipe water damage: the first hour

A burst pipe can release hundreds of gallons in the time it takes to find the main valve. Know where yours is before you need it, and shut it off first, before you start moving belongings.

After the valve is closed, open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain remaining water from the lines. Then photograph the damage and call the 24/7 line so extraction can start.

Pipes inside walls and ceilings leave water in places you cannot see. Extraction handles the floor, and moisture mapping then follows the water into the wall cavities and the room below.

Fixing the pipe itself is a plumber's job. Restoration crews remove the water and dry the structure so the plumber's repair is not covered up by damp walls.

Water in a crawl space

Some homes have a crawl space under a raised floor, and a broken irrigation line or plumbing leak can fill it without anyone seeing. If you hear water running with nothing on, or floors feel soft near the foundation, call.

A crawl space is extracted with pumps and then dried with equipment placed through the access panel. Leaving it wet invites mold on the framing, which is why it should not wait.
