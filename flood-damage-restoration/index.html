If water or sewage is backing up through a floor drain, tub or toilet, stop using water in the house and stay out of the affected area. That is a sewage backup, and it needs cleanup with protective gear, not a mop.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood damage restoration in Fountain Valley covers water that came in from outside, from a storm drain, from a channel, from groundwater or from a sewer line. It is treated differently from a pipe leak because the water is usually contaminated.

Orange County Public Works names the Santa Ana River, which forms Fountain Valley's eastern border, as the county's primary flood threat. Its 1938 flood reportedly killed more than 50 people in nearby cities, and a 1969 storm caused the largest dollar loss in county history.

Prado Dam and later work have reduced that risk. The county also warns that areas outside the river's 100-year zone can still flood from storm drains and channels that lack the capacity for a 100-year flow, so a dry year does not mean your address is safe.

Basement and lower-level flooding cleanup

Many homes in Orange County have no basement, but some have lower-level rooms, sunken areas, garages below grade or crawl spaces where flood water collects first. If your home has one of these, treat it as the first place to check after heavy rain.

Crews pump the water out, remove saturated carpet, pad and drywall, then set drying equipment and monitor until readings are normal. Water that has sat for more than a day or two needs closer attention for mold.

Flood restoration is not waterproofing. If your lower level floods repeatedly, a waterproofing or drainage contractor is the right trade, and we will tell you that honestly.

Sewage backup cleanup

Sewage is category 3 water, and it is the most urgent kind. Crews wear protective gear, extract the liquid, remove carpet, pad and wet drywall that cannot be cleaned, disinfect the surfaces and deodorize.

A backup may come from the public main or from your private lateral. Ask the City of Fountain Valley Public Works whether the blockage is in the public line, because that decides who pays to clear it.

Neighboring Santa Ana says it does not maintain the roughly 46,000 private laterals in its system, and Garden Grove says owners maintain the lateral from home to the public sewer. Do not assume Fountain Valley works the same way, and ask.

Whole-house and crawl space flooding

When water covers most of a floor or fills a crawl space, the job grows into several phases: extraction, removal of ruined materials, structural drying, cleaning and rebuild. Each phase is scoped before it starts.

Large losses also call for careful notes. Your adjuster will want a written scope and photos, and the crew supplies both so your claim can move forward.

Flood insurance: what to check before a storm

The California Department of Insurance says standard homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Flood coverage is usually bought separately through the National Flood Insurance Program, created by Congress in 1968 and run by FEMA, or from a private insurer.

A new NFIP policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, so buying one during a storm is too late. You can look up the flood zone for your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and your agent can explain what your own policy covers.
