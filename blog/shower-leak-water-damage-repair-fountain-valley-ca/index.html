Shower Leak Water Damage Repair in Fountain Valley, CA

A shower can leak at the valve, the pan, the drain or the grout, and each calls for a different repair. Here is how the source gets found.

By the Fountain Valley Water Damage Pros team, 2026

Where shower leaks start

Most shower leaks begin at one of four places: the valve behind the wall, the pan or liner under the tile, the drain connection, or failed grout and caulk. They look alike from the outside, which is why guessing wastes time.

A leak at the face of the tile is usually a nuisance. A leak at the pan or drain can send water into the subfloor and the ceiling below, and that is where the real damage happens.

How the leak gets traced

  1. 1

    Dry test

    Stop using the shower and watch whether readings fall.

  2. 2

    Moisture mapping

    Meters and a thermal camera outline the wet area.

  3. 3

    Targeted test

    Supply pressure or a drain flood test narrows the source.

  4. 4

    Open the wall

    A small opening confirms what is happening inside.

  5. 5

    Plan the repair

    The scope depends on what the water has already touched.

Tile off or spot repair?

If the leak is limited to grout and caulk and the wall behind is dry, regrouting and resealing may be enough. If the pan has failed, the tile usually has to come out so the liner can be repaired or replaced.

Wet drywall, insulation and subfloor are replaced after drying, in that order. A good plan avoids opening more than needed, but it does not leave wet material in place to save a few hours.

When the leak has already reached a floor or ceiling, our emergency water removal crews extract standing water and start drying, and the rebuild follows through water damage repair.

What to do today

  • Stop using the shower until the source is found
  • Shut off the water to the fixture if you can reach the valve
  • Photograph ceiling stains, swollen trim and damp floors
  • Turn off power to a wet room only if you can reach the panel dry
  • Keep towels and fans going on surface moisture, not wall cavities
  • Note any rise in your water bill

Ceiling below the shower? Treat it as urgent

If the bathroom sits above another room, a stain on that ceiling means water has already traveled through the subfloor. Drywall that holds water can sag, and a sagging ceiling is both a safety concern and a sign of larger saturation.

Stay out from under it, keep people and pets away, and call before attempting to poke it open. Water pooled above a ceiling near light fixtures also raises an electrical risk, so cut power to that circuit only if you can reach the panel dry.

A crew can check how far the moisture has spread with a thermal camera, which shows wet areas the eye cannot see.

A note on timing

Dry wood and drywall tolerate a short soaking. Materials that stay wet for long stretches swell, stain and, over time, support growth. The sooner readings start to fall, the smaller the final repair tends to be.

If you rent the home or live in a condo, tell the owner or association in writing the same day, because shared plumbing often sits behind walls you cannot open yourself.

Permits and water in Fountain Valley

Repairs to plumbing systems regulated by the code generally need a permit from the City of Fountain Valley Building Division, which also covers repairs to a building or structure. Its page does not say how this applies to a shower repair, so call the division before work starts and ask.

The city says about 60% of its supply is groundwater from the Lower Santa Ana River Groundwater Basin, 26% is imported and 14% is recycled water used mainly for irrigation. Whatever the source, a sudden jump in your own water bill is one of the better early clues that a leak is running behind a wall.

Sources: City of Fountain Valley Building Division, City of Fountain Valley water supply page
