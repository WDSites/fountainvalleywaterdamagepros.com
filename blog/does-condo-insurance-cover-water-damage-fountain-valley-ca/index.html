Does Condo Insurance Cover Water Damage? A Fountain Valley, CA Guide

Condo claims are split between two policies and sometimes a neighbor. Knowing who owns what helps you act quickly and ask the right questions.

By the Fountain Valley Water Damage Pros team, 2026

Two policies, one wet unit

A condo owner usually deals with two layers of coverage. The association carries a master policy for the building and common areas, and each owner carries an individual policy, often called an HO-6, for the unit's interior and belongings.

Which policy responds depends on what failed, where the water came from and what your governing documents say about the boundary between owner and association. This guide explains the general picture, but your policy and your HOA documents decide the actual answer.

No article can promise that a claim will be covered. What it can do is help you gather the right facts before you call.

When the water comes from the unit above

An upstairs burst pipe is the classic condo scenario. Water travels through floor assemblies, ceilings and walls, and the downstairs owner often has no idea what failed.

Start by stopping further damage. Our emergency water removal crews extract standing water and begin drying, which reduces the secondary damage your insurer will care about. Then report the loss to your insurer and tell the association manager so the source can be found.

Exchange contact details with the neighbor above, but avoid arguing about fault at the door. Photographs and a written account do more than a verbal dispute.

Documenting the loss

What to gather

  • Dated photos and video of the water, the source and every affected room
  • A list of damaged belongings with approximate purchase dates
  • The name of the building manager and the time you reported the leak
  • Copies of your HO-6 declarations page and the HOA governing documents
  • Receipts for emergency services, hotel stays and temporary repairs
  • A written description of what you did to limit the damage

Questions to put to your insurer

What is my deductible for water losses?

Some policies use a different deductible for certain perils, so confirm the number before work starts.

Is the cause sudden or gradual?

Sudden and accidental events are often treated differently from long-term seepage, and the wording in your policy matters.

Which parts of the unit are mine?

Ask whether cabinets, flooring and drywall fall under your policy or the association's.

What does loss of use include?

If the unit is unlivable, find out what temporary housing the policy may cover and how receipts are submitted.

Write the answers down with the date and the name of the person who gave them. A short written record prevents confusion weeks later.

Flood is a separate question in California

The California Department of Insurance states that standard homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, mudslide, debris flow and similar disasters. A burst pipe is different from a flood, but if rising water from a storm enters a ground-floor unit, a standard condo policy may not respond.

Flood coverage comes through the National Flood Insurance Program, created by Congress in 1968 and run by FEMA, or from private insurers. The department notes that a new NFIP policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, so buying one after a storm warning is too late.

Mitigation is your job, even when coverage is unclear

Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Water left in a unit for days invites mold and warped floors, which can complicate a claim.

A water damage cleanup crew removes wet materials, sanitizes and documents the work so the paper trail is clear. Condo owners in Huntington Beach and Santa Ana use the same process when a unit floods.

Ask your insurer in writing what they need and when. Keep every message.
