Does Renters Insurance Cover Water Damage in Fountain Valley, CA?

It often covers your belongings after a sudden leak, but not the building. Here is how to think about it as a renter, and what to do while the water is still spreading.

By the Fountain Valley Water Damage Pros team, 2026

The split: your things, their building

A renters policy generally protects your personal property, such as furniture, clothing and electronics, and may help with extra living costs if the unit is not livable. It does not usually pay for the building itself. The structure, walls, ceilings and fixed plumbing belong to the landlord, and the landlord's insurance deals with them.

The cause of the water matters as much as the damage. Policies commonly respond to sudden and accidental events, such as a burst pipe or an overflowing appliance. Slow leaks and neglect are often treated differently, and flood is usually excluded entirely.

Your own policy has the final word, so read the declarations page and the water damage section, or ask your insurer directly.

If water is still coming in

Stop the source

Shut the valve under the fixture or ask the landlord to shut off the building supply.

Keep power safe

Only cut power to wet areas if you can reach the panel while standing dry.

Lift what you can

Move electronics, papers and small furniture onto dry surfaces.

Tell the landlord

Report in writing right away and keep a copy of the message.

Document the loss before you clean

  • Photos and short videos of each wet room and item
  • A list of damaged belongings with rough purchase details
  • The date and time you noticed water and who you told
  • Receipts for hotel stays, meals or drying supplies
  • Messages with the landlord and any property manager
  • The name and claim number of anyone you speak with at your insurer

Cleanup in a rented unit

Landlords often arrange structural drying themselves. Your belongings are separate, and soaked carpet, rugs and upholstered items need attention quickly. If you want help with water damage cleanup for your own contents, ask the landlord who is responsible for the floor and walls first.

When water is actively spreading, emergency water removal is the first priority, and the landlord's insurer can sort out costs afterward. Renters in Costa Mesa and Santa Ana call us about this often, because many nearby households rent.

Questions to ask your insurer in the first call

Ask whether the loss falls under a sudden and accidental event, and what documentation the insurer wants. Ask how the deductible applies and whether loss-of-use coverage kicks in if the unit is not livable.

Request the claim number in writing, and ask what the insurer expects you to do to protect your belongings from further damage. Reasonable steps, like moving items off wet floors, are normally expected of policyholders.

Keep a log of every call with the date, the person and what was said. If the answer on any point is unclear, ask for it by email so you have a record to point back to.

Finally, ask whether your policy has a time limit for reporting a loss. Prompt notice protects your claim, and it also protects your belongings, since wet items lose value quickly.

Renting in and around Fountain Valley

About 68.6% of Fountain Valley's housing units are owner-occupied, according to Census figures, which means roughly a third are not. Next door in Costa Mesa the 2020 split was the other way around, with 61.1% of occupied units rented. Either way, many households in this area carry renters coverage and not a homeowner policy.

On flood specifically, the California Department of Insurance notes that standard homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. The National Flood Insurance Program, created in 1968 and run by FEMA, offers separate coverage for renters in participating communities. A new NFIP policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, so it is not something to buy after the water arrives.
