Where We Work

Our home base is Fountain Valley, California, in Orange County. From there crews reach the neighboring cities that share a border or a freeway with Fountain Valley, which keeps travel short when water is spreading.

The Santa Ana River forms Fountain Valley's eastern border, and Interstate 405 crosses the city, so most of the nearby towns below are a short drive away. Each one has its own page with ZIP codes and local housing notes.

Crews know the freeway on-ramps and the main boulevards, and they plan routes around traffic when a call comes in. If your address is just outside these towns, call anyway. The service area is a guide, and the dispatcher can confirm availability by ZIP code.

Choosing the Right Page

If you live in one of these towns, start with its page, because it explains housing, drainage and ZIP coverage in plain terms. If you live in Fountain Valley itself, start with the homepage and then the service that matches your loss.

Renters and condo owners can read the town pages too, but should also tell the dispatcher about their living situation, since landlords and associations usually handle part of the response.

How Dispatch Decides Who Goes First

When several calls arrive at once, active water comes before everything else. A burst pipe or a sewage backup gets a crew ahead of a stain that appeared last week, because the first one is still getting worse.

The dispatcher will ask whether water is still flowing, whether it is near electricity and whether anyone is at risk. Those answers shape the order, and they are the reason the first call should include as much detail as you can safely give.

If your loss is not urgent, say so. A calm description of a slow leak or an old stain lets the dispatcher schedule an inspection without pulling a crew away from someone who needs one now.

What Is the Same Everywhere

The process does not change from town to town. Dispatch answers the line, the crew explains the scope and price before work starts, and the work follows the same steps from water mitigation through water damage repair.

What does change is the housing stock, the drainage and the utilities. Each town page covers what is particular to that place, and the services overview explains the work itself.
