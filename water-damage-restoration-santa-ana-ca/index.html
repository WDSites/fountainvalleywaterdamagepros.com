When water is spreading in Santa Ana, density changes the plan

Think in buildings, not rooms

Santa Ana has about 11,350 people per square mile, the densest city on our service list. In crowded blocks, a leak in one unit often reaches walls, ceilings and floors in the next, so we ask for the building layout, not just your door number.

Tell dispatch whether you share walls, whether anyone is below you and where the building shutoff is. Our emergency water removal team then plans extraction with shared structure in mind.

The river, the dams and the 1938 flood

The Santa Ana River runs through the west end of the city. It is mostly channelized and largely seasonal because of Prado Dam and Seven Oaks Dam, but it caused several severe 20th-century floods. OC Public Works calls it the county's primary flood threat and says a 1938 flood affected parts of Anaheim, Santa Ana and Garden Grove and reportedly killed more than 50 people.

A 1969 storm caused the largest dollar loss in county history, even with Prado Dam in place. OC Public Works also says some areas outside the river's 100-year inundation zone could flood from storm drains and channels that lack capacity. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage, since standard policies usually exclude it. Our flood damage restoration crews handle the cleanup when it happens.

Downtown, Midtown and Civic Center buildings

Santa Ana has several districts. Downtown, the Civic Center, Midtown along Main Street, Metro East, South Coast Metro and the Historic South Main Business District each carry different building types. Some downtown homes date to the late 1800s, and the Old Orange County Courthouse is a reminder of how old parts of the city are.

Older homes may hide older materials in walls, so we scan before opening. The Bowers Museum, the Santa Ana Zoo and Discovery Cube Orange County bring visitors and staff into larger buildings, where a leak in a ceiling can affect exhibits or offices. If you manage a larger building, tell dispatch about access and after-hours contacts.

On a house call, a thermal camera and moisture meter show how far water traveled before anyone cuts into a wall. That scan keeps the scope honest and keeps repairs from running past what the water actually reached.
